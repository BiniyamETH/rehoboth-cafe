First Visit?

New to Ethiopian food?

Ethiopian meals are made for sharing. Here is what to expect at the table.

  1. Injera is your plate and spoon

    Injera is a soft, slightly sour flatbread traditionally made from teff. Dishes are served on top of it, with extra rolls on the side.

  2. Eat with your right hand

    Tear off a piece of injera and use it to scoop up the food. Cutlery is never wrong, though.

  3. Share from one platter

    Order a few dishes for the table. Offering a friend a bite by hand, called gursha, is a sign of warmth.

  4. Vegetarian dishes are a tradition

    Many Ethiopians eat meat-free on fasting days, so dishes like Shiro and Misir Wot are classics, not an afterthought.

The Ethiopian coffee ceremony

Ethiopia is widely known as the birthplace of coffee. At home, coffee is a ritual of hospitality: beans are roasted, brewed and shared with family and friends, one slow round at a time.

  1. 1

    Roast

    Green coffee beans are washed and roasted over a small flame until dark and fragrant, then passed around so everyone can enjoy the aroma.

  2. 2

    Brew

    The beans are ground and boiled in a jebena, a clay coffee pot, often with incense burning nearby.

  3. 3

    Share

    Coffee is poured into small cups over three rounds: abol, tona and baraka, the last said to bring a blessing.
